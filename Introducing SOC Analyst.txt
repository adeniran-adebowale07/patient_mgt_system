Course Title: Before the Console: Mindset & Foundations for Future SOC Analysts

Total Time: 2 hours
Target Audience: Aspiring SOC Analysts (Career changers, students, IT pros looking to pivot)
Goal: To build the fundamental IT knowledge, critical thinking habits, and personal projects that will help them get hired.

---

Part 1: The "How" Before the "What" (25 mins)

· (5 mins) Welcome & The Goal
  · Instructor introduction: From "civilian" to analyst.
  · The reality: You don't need to be a hacker yet. You need to be a curious investigator.
  · Objective: Bridge the gap between "I want to be in security" and "I am ready for an interview."
· (20 mins) The Analyst Mindset (Soft Skills)
  · Curiosity: The difference between "The alert fired" and "Why did it fire?"
  · Attention to Detail: Spotting the anomaly in the noise (e.g., a 1 vs a l in a domain name).
  · Written Communication: You will live in a ticketing system. Practice writing clear, concise English. (Exercise: Describe what you did last weekend in 3 sentences without emotion).
  · Humility: You will be wrong. You will close false positives. Learning to admit you don't know and ask for help is a strength.

---

Part 2: The IT Prerequisites (What you must know) (45 mins)

· (15 mins) Networking Fundamentals (The Traffic)
  · The OSI Model in Practice: Don't memorize the numbers, understand the layers. (IP is the address, TCP/UDP is the delivery method, HTTP/HTTPS is the language).
  · Core Protocols:
    · DNS: The phonebook of the internet. (Why do attackers love messing with it?)
    · HTTP/HTTPS: The web. Understanding the difference between a GET request and a POST request (uploading data).
    · Ports: Not just numbers. 22=SSH (admin), 3389=RDP (remote desktop), 443=HTTPS (web).
  · Analogy: The network is the road system. You need to know the roads before you can spot the getaway car.
· (15 mins) Operating Systems (The Cars)
  · Windows: The most common target. Understand the difference between a Process (an app running) and a Service (running in the background). What is svchost.exe?
  · Linux: The backbone of servers. You don't need to be a sysadmin, but you need to be comfortable looking at a command line.
  · File Systems: Where do things live? (e.g., Windows: Temp folders are often hiding spots for malware).
· (15 mins) Core Security Concepts
  · The CIA Triad:
    · Confidentiality: Keeping secrets secret (Encryption).
    · Integrity: Making sure data isn't tampered with (Hashing).
    · Availability: Making sure it's there when you need it (Backups/DDOS).
  · The Difference:
    · Vulnerability: A hole in the wall (a missing patch).
    · Threat: Someone looking for a hole (a hacker).
    · Risk: The chance that someone finds the hole and crawls through.

---

Part 3: Building Your Arsenal (Personal Projects) (30 mins)

· (10 mins) Setting Up a Home Lab
  · Why: You cannot defend what you do not understand.
  · The Setup:
    · VirtualBox/VMware: Free software to run "pretend" computers.
    · The Targets: Install a old Windows 10 VM and a Linux (Ubuntu) VM.
    · The Looker: Install Wireshark to "see" the network traffic these VMs create.
  · Task: Just watch what your own computer does when you open Google.com in Wireshark.
· (10 mins) Learning to Google (The Real Skill)
  · The Problem: You will see errors you don't recognize 100 times a day.
  · The Skill:
    · How to copy/paste an error message effectively.
    · How to filter results by date (recent articles are often better).
    · How to use " quotes " to search for exact strings.
  · Exercise: "I just saw Event ID 4688 on my PC. What does that mean?" (Spoiler: It means a process started).
· (10 mins) Resources for Continuous Learning
  · Free Stuff: TryHackMe (SOC Fundamentals path), YouTube (John Hammond, IppSec), MITRE ATT&CK framework (the "encyclopedia" of hacker behavior).
  · Follow the News: Why reading breach reports (Uber, LastPass, MGM) is like reading case studies for your future job.

---

Part 4: The Roadmap (20 mins)

· (10 mins) Certifications vs. Experience
  · Entry Level: Security+ (Great for HR filters), CySA+ (More analyst focused).
  · The Catch: A certificate gets the interview; a home lab and curiosity get the job.
  · Mapping Skills: How the IT stuff we discussed (Networking, OS) maps directly to SOC work.
· (10 mins) Q&A & Final Advice
  · Open floor for questions.
  · Call to Action:
    1. Install VirtualBox tonight.
    2. Create a free TryHackMe account and do the "Pre-Security" path.
    3. Write down one thing you learned today and research it further.
  · Handout: "The Aspiring SOC Analyst Reading List" (Links to blogs, podcasts, and free labs).
