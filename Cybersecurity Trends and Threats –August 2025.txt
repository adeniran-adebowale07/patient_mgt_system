Cybersecurity Trends and Threats – Zambia (August 1–30, 2025)
In August 2025, Zambia was directly implicated in Operation Serengeti 2.0, a coordinated African cybercrime crackdown. While no critical infrastructure breaches were publicly disclosed, the scope of the operation revealed deep vulnerabilities in Zambia’s cybersecurity ecosystem especially in terms of infrastructure exposure to crypto scams, identity fraud, and financial manipulation.
4.1. Key Threats and Incidents
• As part of Operation Serengeti 2.0 (June–August 2025), Zambian law enforcement dismantled a major cryptocurrency investment scam affecting ~65,000 victims and leading to ~US $300 million in losses. Authorities arrested 15 suspects and seized key infrastructure including fake domains, mobile SIMs, and financial accounts tied to fraudulent operations.
• The scam was part of a broader syndicate operating across 18 African countries, showing Zambia’s centrality in regional digital fraud networks.
4.2. Regional Spillover and Infrastructure Risks
• INTERPOL revealed the Zambian operation was linked to a broader network involving forged documents (372 fake passports) and possible human trafficking, suggesting an overlap between cybercrime and transnational organized crime.
• These operations typically exploit digital infrastructure gaps, including unsecured telecom systems, weak KYC enforcement in fintechs, and poor surveillance integration across critical infrastructure sectors like banking and identity services.

4.3. Government and Infrastructure Response
• Although no new cyber policy was enacted in August, authorities acknowledged that Zambia’s ICT and law enforcement infrastructure remains insufficiently integrated. Investigators leveraged assistance from African CERTs, telecom regulators, and Interpol’s Cybercrime Directorate, indicating reliance on foreign and regional threat intelligence and digital forensics capacity.
• Operation Serengeti 2.0 exposed the need for infrastructure-level modernization: Zambia lacks a unified cyber response framework across telecoms, banks, border control, and digital identity management. The operation catalyzed informal coordination across agencies, but experts say formal investment in threat detection systems, central SOCs (Security Operations Centers), and multi-sector KYC enforcement is now urgent.
